# Cloud-Aware Super-Resolution for Generating Historical AlphaEarth-Like Imagery from Landsat

## Theoretical Framework, Algorithmic Design, and Experimental Validation

---

## Abstract

This report presents a cloud-aware, time-aware super-resolution framework for reconstructing AlphaEarth-like high-dimensional representations from historical Landsat 8 observations. The target problem is a cross-sensor, cross-resolution, and cross-representation mapping task: low-resolution physical optical measurements are translated into high-resolution semantic embeddings. The proposed design combines a memory-efficient SwinIR backbone with late upsampling, explicit Day-of-Year (DOY) conditioning, learnable temporal positional encoding, and soft cloud masking; we additionally explore, but do not adopt by default, a cloud cross-attention variant. Experimental validation on a localized smoke-test footprint (Landsat Path 132/Row 33, 2018) demonstrates consistent performance improvements: the best configuration (ablation_4f) achieves PSNR = 14.7599 dB (+0.5109 dB vs. baseline), SSIM = 0.3889, ERGAS = 10.7375, and SAM = 0.3385. The manuscript emphasizes mathematical consistency, implementation-aligned tensor flow, reproducibility, and a comprehensive failure mode analysis.

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Shift Toward High-Dimensional Geospatial Representations

Earth observation (EO) analysis is shifting from relying on raw physical spectral reflectance bands to utilizing multi-modal geospatial foundation models. AlphaEarth Foundations (AEF) exemplifies this shift by mapping spatial, temporal, and measurement contexts into a unified 64-dimensional semantic space (Brown et al., 2025). Unlike traditional spectral indices that reduce measurements to single scalars, this 64-dimensional abstraction encodes environmental properties on a unit hypersphere ($S^{63}$), yielding continuous features at a 10-meter spatial resolution (Google DeepMind, 2025). This representation demonstrates quantifiable efficacy in downstream tasks, including fine-grained crop type mapping and change detection.

### 1.2 The Historical Data Gap

The operational planetary-scale embeddings generated by AEF commence primarily in 2017, aligning with the global availability of Sentinel-2 data (Brown et al., 2025). However, longitudinal environmental analyses, such as decadal deforestation or long-term urban encroachment, require observational baselines extending back to the early 2010s. The Landsat 8 satellite archive, operational since 2013, provides the temporal continuity necessary to retroactively extend AEF-equivalent feature spaces.

### 1.3 The Ill-Posed Nature of the Translation Task

Mapping a low-resolution (LR) sequence of 9 optical bands at 30m spatial resolution to a high-resolution (HR) target of 64 semantic dimensions at 10m resolution constitutes a highly ill-posed inverse problem. The complexity arises from three factors:

- **Information Rate Imbalance**: Expanding a 9-dimensional physical space into a 64-dimensional semantic space requires non-linear inference of latent physical properties not explicitly captured by raw Landsat optics.
- **Temporal Asynchrony**: Landsat 8 operates on a 16-day orbital revisit cycle. Cloud-induced data gaps render the resulting Satellite Image Time Series (SITS) highly asynchronous.
- **Irregular Cloud Contamination**: Standard preprocessing algorithms rely on binary (0/1) hard masks that discard all contaminated pixels, eradicating attenuated but viable reflectance signals present beneath thin clouds or shadow boundaries (Pan, 2020).

### 1.4 Proposed Algorithmic Design

To stabilize the inversion process, this research develops a spatiotemporal, cloud-aware super-resolution architecture integrating three mechanisms:

1. **Continuous Time Band Encoding**: Injection of Day-of-Year (DOY) temporal matrices to condition the convolutional backbone on seasonal phenology.
2. **Learnable Positional Encoding**: A parameterized temporal embedding that adaptively learns dataset-specific temporal periodicities.
3. **Soft Cloud Masking and Optional Cloud Cross-Attention**: Continuous cloud probability maps are used as soft guidance signals; Section 4.3 further explores an explicit cross-attention variant, which is not part of the current best-performing configuration.

## 2. Literature Review

### 2.1 High-Dimensional Representation and Cross-Sensor Super-Resolution

Recent foundation models extract generalized representations via self-supervised learning, such as SatMAE (Cong et al., 2022) and SatCLIP (Klemmer et al., 2025). AEF shifts this paradigm from patch-level embeddings to dense, pixel-level continuous fields at 10m resolution (Brown et al., 2025).

To spatially enhance Landsat data to Sentinel-2 or AEF resolutions, cross-sensor super-resolution (CSSR) methodologies have transitioned from geostatistical downscaling (e.g., ATPRK; Wang et al., 2016) to deep learning. For instance, DTGAN introduced dilated convolutions into Transformer self-attention modules to capture multi-scale textural details during 30m to 10m mapping (Wang et al., 2023). However, single-image architectures struggle to resolve spectral ambiguity when mapping physical bands to high-dimensional semantic spaces without temporal context.

### 2.2 Rationale for the SwinIR Backbone

This study selects Swin Image Restoration (SwinIR) (Liang et al., 2021) as the core spatial backbone rather than standard SRCNN or global Vision Transformers. The selection is driven by three architectural advantages:

- **Shifted-Window Self-Attention**: Remote sensing imagery exhibits high spatial heterogeneity. Localized window attention prevents the oversmoothing of diverse land covers commonly seen in global attention networks.
- **Memory Efficiency**: Global attention scales quadratically $O(N^2)$, which is computationally prohibitive for multi-temporal ($T=20$) inputs. SwinIR's linear complexity $O(N)$ allows the network to process long sequences efficiently.
- **Extensibility**: The modular Residual Swin Transformer Blocks (RSTB) are highly compatible with our proposed temporal conditioning and cloud-mask cross-attention extensions.

### 2.3 Asynchronous Time-Series Modeling

Traditional SITS modeling forces asynchronous data into equidistant grids. Harmonic models (e.g., CCDC) fit time series to predefined functions, compressing observations into trend and phase coefficients (Zhu & Woodcock, 2014). While efficient for temporal extraction, they discard high-frequency spatial anomalies. Recently, AnytimeFormer demonstrated that treating continuous observation timestamps as explicit input features allows Transformers to adaptively align asynchronous observations without external resampling (Tang et al., 2026).

### 2.4 Cloud Contamination and Soft Attention

Conventional masking relies on Fmask to generate binary quality indicators, leading to severe data starvation. Soft-masking techniques generate continuous 2D probability maps to retain structural details. The Spatial Attention Generative Adversarial Network (SpA-GAN) employs spatial attention to apply negative residual compensation to cloud layers while preserving underlying geometry (Pan, 2020). This research synthesizes this paradigm by utilizing soft probability masks as an active attention constraint within the SwinIR blocks.

## 3. Study Area and Data Engineering

### 3.1 Study Area Specifications

The methodology is tested on a spatially diverse footprint anchored to Landsat Path 132, Row 33 (Jinchang City, Gansu Province, China) covering the calendar year 2018 (Jiao et al., 2019). This region exhibits a drastic topoclimatic gradient encompassing mountains, deserts, and irrigated dual-cropping agricultural lands. The region maintains a mean annual cloud cover of ~35%, providing an optimal distribution of clear, semi-transparent, and dense cloud observations.

**Smoke-Test Scale:**
- Spatial extent: 3840m × 3840m central block (2 × 2 geographic partition for leakage prevention)
- Temporal coverage: 2018 calendar year with $T=20$ valid observations post-quality filtering
- LR resolution: 64 × 64 pixels (@30m Landsat)
- HR resolution: 192 × 192 pixels (@10m target grid)

### 3.2 Data Pipeline and Leakage Prevention

Processing $T=20$ temporal phases of 64-dimensional AEF target tensors requires a highly optimized data pipeline to prevent data leakage and memory exhaustion.

**Spatial Splitting for Leakage Prevention**: To strictly prevent spatial autocorrelation bias and temporal leakage across phases, the central 3840m x 3840m footprint is geographically partitioned into a $2 \times 2$ grid. The training, validation, and test patches are strictly isolated geographically. Cross-season leakage is mitigated by ensuring that validation patches are evaluated holistically across the entire time series, rather than splitting the time series itself.

In the current smoke-test setup, the split is performed at the tile level on non-overlapping 64 \times 64 LR patches. The processed dataset contains 96,310 training tiles, 48,514 validation tiles, and 43,809 test tiles. Each tile retains up to $T=20$ valid temporal observations after QA filtering.

The split counts above are reported at the processed-tile level. They are large because each geographic tile contributes multiple temporal examples after QA-based sequence construction, so the reported counts should not be interpreted as the number of unique spatial locations.

**Online Dequantization**: AEF data is natively compressed as uint8 arrays. The pipeline applies real-time dequantization during the DataLoader fetching phase: $F_{32} = (I_{uint8} - 127.0) / 127.5$, recovering the 32-bit floating-point distribution required for gradient computation (Brown et al., 2025).

**Landsat Radiometric Scaling**: Landsat 8 Collection 2 Level-1 Top-of-Atmosphere (TOA) digital numbers (DN) are dynamically converted to TOA reflectance using specific multiplicative ($M_L = 2.0 \times 10^{-5}$) and additive ($A_L = -0.1$) scale factors provided in the official metadata (USGS, 2021).

### 3.3 Precise Soft Mask Generation from QA_PIXEL

The architecture abandons traditional binary Fmask thresholding. The Landsat QA_PIXEL band encodes confidence scores in specific bit pairs. The pipeline extracts the 2-bit confidence levels for Cloud (Bits 8-9), Cloud Shadow (Bits 10-11), and Cirrus (Bits 14-15). These bit pairs map to integer states $\{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ denoting None, Low, Medium, and High confidence.

A deterministic mapping function generates a continuous probability tensor $M_{soft} \in [0.0, 1.0]$:

$$C_{class} \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\} \rightarrow \{0.0, 0.33, 0.66, 1.0\}$$

$$M_{soft} = \max(C_{cloud}, C_{shadow}, C_{cirrus})$$

This formulation ensures semi-transparent cirrus clouds (e.g., $M_{soft} = 0.33$) are not unconditionally discarded, allowing the network to extract attenuated reflectance patterns.

The linear mapping $\{0, 1, 2, 3\} \rightarrow \{0.0, 0.33, 0.66, 1.0\}$ is used here as a simple heuristic to reflect increasing cloud confidence. We treat this as an engineering choice rather than a physically derived calibration; future work can either learn the mapping end-to-end or calibrate it against external cloud optical-depth estimates.

## 4. Methodology and Algorithmic Design

### 4.0 Problem Formulation and Loss Functions

We formulate the task as a conditional reconstruction problem. Given a temporal sequence of Landsat observations, soft cloud masks, and acquisition timestamps,

$$\{(L_t, M_t, t_t)\}_{t=1}^{T}, \quad L_t \in \mathbb{R}^{C_{in} \times H_{LR} \times W_{LR}}, \quad M_t \in [0,1]^{1 \times H_{LR} \times W_{LR}}, \quad t_t \in [1,365],$$

the model predicts a fused high-resolution AEF-like target

$$\hat{Y} = f_{\theta}(\{L_t, M_t, t_t\}_{t=1}^{T}) \in \mathbb{R}^{64 \times H_{HR} \times W_{HR}}.$$

Internally, the encoder produces a per-time latent sequence $\{Z_t\}_{t=1}^{T}$ before temporal fusion, and $\hat{Y}$ is obtained only after aggregation. This keeps the final prediction object single-valued while still allowing a temporal consistency term to operate on intermediate representations.

The main reconstruction objective is the configured mixed loss

$$\mathcal{L}_{rec} = \lambda_{L2}\,\mathcal{L}_{L2} + \lambda_{SSIM}\,\mathcal{L}_{SSIM},$$

where $\mathcal{L}_{L2}$ is the pixel-wise regression term and $\mathcal{L}_{SSIM} = 1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{Y}, Y)$ encourages structural fidelity.

All SwinIR-based configurations, including the true baseline and ablations 3b–4f, use this mixed objective. The SRCNN reference is trained under its original baseline recipe and is reported as a lightweight comparison model rather than as part of the SwinIR loss ablation. We note that this may slightly disadvantage SRCNN relative to the mixed-loss variants, so it should be interpreted as a reference baseline rather than a fully optimized competitor.

For the temporal-loss variant (Ablation 4d), we add a consistency term across adjacent latent states:

$$\mathcal{L}_{temp} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{P}|} \sum_{(t, t') \in \mathcal{P}} \left\| Z_t - Z_{t'} \right\|_1,$$

where $\mathcal{P}=\{(t,t+1)\mid t=1,\dots,T-1\}$ denotes adjacent time pairs in the available sequence. The total loss is

$$\mathcal{L}_{total} = \mathcal{L}_{rec} + \lambda_{temp}\,\mathcal{L}_{temp}.$$

In the current smoke-test settings, $\lambda_{L2}=1.0$, $\lambda_{SSIM}=1.0$, and $\lambda_{temp}$ is enabled only for the 4d variant according to its training configuration.

### 4.1 Single Source of Truth: Tensor Dimension Flow

To resolve any ambiguity regarding the spatial and temporal processing pathways, Table 1 documents the precise tensor shape transformations through the complete forward pass. The configuration assumes batch size $B=4$, temporal phases $T=20$, optical bands $C_{in}=9$, embedding dimension $D=180$, target $C_{out}=64$, and an LR patch size of $64 \times 64$.

| Processing Module | Input Shape | Output Shape | Algorithmic Operation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data Ingestion | $(B, T, 9, 64, 64)$ | $(B \times T, 9, 64, 64)$ | Flatten temporal dimension for shared 2D convolution |
| Temporal Injection | $(B \times T, 9, 64, 64)$ | $(B \times T, 11, 64, 64)$ | Channel concatenation of a 2-channel DOY time_band |
| Shallow Extraction | $(B \times T, 11, 64, 64)$ | $(B \times T, 180, 64, 64)$ | $3 \times 3$ Convolution mapping to embedding dim $D=180$ |
| SwinIR Body | $(B \times T, 180, 64, 64)$ | $(B \times T, 180, 64, 64)$ | Residual Swin Transformer Blocks (RSTB) computation |
| Cloud Cross-Attn | $(B \times T, 180, 64, 64)$ | $(B \times T, 180, 64, 64)$ | Soft-mask guided Multi-Head Cross-Attention |
| Temporal Fusion | $(B \times T, 180, 64, 64)$ | $(B, 180, 64, 64)$ | Reshape to $(B, T,...)$ and apply mean/attention pooling |
| Late Upsampling | $(B, 180, 64, 64)$ | $(B, 64, 192, 192)$ | Sub-pixel Conv (PixelShuffle) mapping to $3\times$ HR grid |

**Critical Observation**: All computationally heavy self-attention modules operate exclusively at the low-resolution grid ($64 \times 64$). Spatial expansion is strictly deferred to the Late Upsampling block, preventing memory exhaustion and enabling efficient temporal aggregation.

**Architecture Scope Note**: Table 1 shows the superset architecture. In the best-performing configuration (4f), the Cloud Cross-Attn block is bypassed and the SwinIR body output is routed directly into temporal fusion; the ablation study in Section 5.1 toggles this block on or off depending on the configuration.

### 4.2 Explicit Time Injection

The acquisition Day-of-Year ($t_i \in [1, 365]$) must be encoded to interpret asynchronous phenological variations. In the current implementation, we use a scalar DOY conditioning signal expanded into two channels via sine and cosine, which then concatenates with the nine Landsat bands to form the 11-channel input in Table 1.

$$\phi_{time}(t_i) = \left[\sin\left(\frac{2\pi t_i}{365}\right), \cos\left(\frac{2\pi t_i}{365}\right)\right]$$

For the learnable variant used in Ablations 4e and 4f, we parameterize a learnable frequency $\alpha$ and phase $\beta$ as scalars:

$$\phi_{learn}(t_i)=\left[\sin\left(\alpha\,\frac{2\pi t_i}{365}+\beta\right),\cos\left(\alpha\,\frac{2\pi t_i}{365}+\beta\right)\right]$$

with $\alpha$ initialized to $1$ and $\beta$ initialized to $0$. This 2-D embedding is broadcast over the spatial grid and concatenated with the 9 Landsat bands, resulting in the 11 channels shown in Table 1.

This learnable mechanism allows the network to automatically adjust its temporal frequency to local agricultural cycles, superseding rigid, fixed harmonic constraints. The additional degree of freedom enables the model to capture dataset-specific periodicities beyond the canonical 365-day cycle (e.g., multi-year trends or monthly management cycles).

The fixed and learnable forms are therefore consistent: the fixed encoding provides a deterministic baseline, while the learnable variant keeps the same two-channel interface but replaces the fixed frequency with trainable parameters.

### 4.3 Cloud Mask Cross-Attention (Exploratory Variant)

Note: The cross-attention formulation described in this section corresponds to an exploratory variant (Ablation 3b), which does not constitute the best-performing configuration (4f) in our current experiments. We keep it here as a design study rather than the default backbone.

Heavy cloud cover artificially suppresses the internal variance of the optical feature map, causing standard self-attention to fail. We elevate the soft probability mask $M_{soft}$ into a spatial routing variable using Multi-Head Cross-Attention.

The Query ($Q$) is derived from the projected cloud mask, while the Key ($K$) and Value ($V$) are derived from the optical feature maps. To explicitly penalize the network for querying heavily occluded regions, an additive mask bias $B_{mask}$ is defined using a critical opacity threshold $\tau$:

$$B_{mask}(i, j) = \begin{cases} 0 & \text{if } M_{soft}(j) < \tau \\ -\infty & \text{if } M_{soft}(j) \ge \tau \end{cases}$$

The physically-guided Cloud Cross-Attention computation is executed as:

$$\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{Softmax}\left( \frac{Q K^T}{\sqrt{d_k}} + B_{mask} \right) V$$

By adding $B_{mask}$ to the scaled dot-product before the Softmax normalization, the attention weights for clouded Key regions are mathematically forced to zero. The network is compelled to route information from adjacent clear temporal frames.

### 4.3.1 Prob-OR Mask Fusion

Given a temporal sequence of soft masks $\{M_t\}_{t=1}^{T}$, we define the probabilistic OR fusion as

$$M_{OR}(x,y) = 1 - \prod_{t=1}^{T} \left(1 - M_t(x,y)\right).$$

This corresponds to the probability that at least one observation at location $(x,y)$ is affected by cloud or shadow. In the current implementation, this fused mask is used as a soft guidance signal rather than a hard exclusion rule.

### 4.4 Temporal Fusion Strategy

The current best-performing configuration (4f) uses simple mean pooling across the temporal dimension after feature refinement. We therefore make the fusion rule explicit here instead of leaving it as an ambiguous "mean/attention pooling" placeholder.

$$F_{fused}(x,y) = \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^{T} F_t(x,y),$$

where $\{F_t\}_{t=1}^{T}$ are the per-time latent features and the valid set is the full available sequence, i.e., no hard timestamp dropping is performed in the mainline model. Soft cloud handling happens through mask-guided feature modulation rather than by removing cloudy observations. If a temporal attention variant is used in an ablation, it should be reported as a separate configuration rather than merged into the main 4f description.

## 5. Experimental Design and Evaluation Framework

### 5.1 Baseline Definitions and Ablation Strategy

To prove the efficacy of the proposed spatiotemporal architecture, performance is benchmarked against established standard networks and specific ablation states:

- **SRCNN**: Baseline for standard 2D spatial convolution without temporal aggregation (Dong et al., 2016).
- **True Baseline (SwinIR Late-Upsample)**: The SwinIR backbone adapted for $T=20$ sequence inputs, utilizing late upsampling but omitting all time and mask embedding features (Liang et al., 2021).
- **Ablation 3b (3b_with_cross_attention_posenc_learnable)**: SwinIR with Cross-Attention and Learnable PosEnc, but without mask-based strategies.
- **Ablation 4c (4c_mask_reduce_prob_or)**: Introduces Prob-OR mask fusion strategy.
- **Ablation 4d (4d_mask_temporal_loss)**: Prob-OR masking with temporal consistency regularization.
- **Ablation 4e (4e_mask_prob_or_learnable_pos)**: Prob-OR + Learnable Positional Encoding.
- **Ablation 4f (4f_mask_prob_or_learnable_pos_no_cross)**: Prob-OR + Learnable PosEnc without extra cross-branch (Current Best).

### 5.2 Evaluation Metrics

The reconstruction quality of the 64-dimensional semantic space is assessed using both spatial and spectral metrics:

- **PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio)**: Measures absolute pixel-wise reconstruction error, computed as $\text{PSNR}=10\log_{10}\left(\frac{\text{MAX}^2}{\text{MSE}}\right)$ with MAX=1.0.
- **SSIM (Structural Similarity Index)**: Evaluates structural and textural preservation across the 10m grid, computed per-band then averaged (Reyes & Vargas, 2022).
- **ERGAS (Erreur Relative Globale Adimensionnelle de Synthèse)**: Quantifies global relative dimensionless error for multi-band imagery, sensitive to spectral preservation.
- **SAM (Spectral Angle Mapper)**: Determines the geometric angle between the predicted 64-dimensional vector and the ground truth vector, capturing semantic feature fidelity independent of magnitude (Romero et al., 2020).

**Metric Scale Note**: The absolute PSNR/SSIM values in this task are not directly comparable to conventional RGB super-resolution benchmarks, because we operate in a 64-dimensional semantic embedding space rather than three-color images. The main claim is therefore relative improvement over the strong SwinIR baseline, not absolute metric magnitude.

**Statistical Significance Protocol**: This midterm smoke-test uses a fixed random seed (seed=42) for all reported runs. We report single-run results here; multi-seed mean ± standard deviation will be added in the final report if additional training budget becomes available.

### 5.4 Key Findings

**Finding 1: Configuration 4f Achieves the Best Performance Among All Tested Configurations on the Smoke-Test Footprint**

The proposed full pipeline (ablation_4f: Prob-OR mask fusion + Learnable Positional Encoding without extra cross-branch) achieves:
- PSNR: 14.7599 dB (+0.5109 dB over baseline)
- SSIM: 0.3889 (highest structural preservation)
- ERGAS: 10.7375 (lowest global spectral error)
- SAM: 0.3385 (lowest spectral angle deviation)

**Finding 2: Temporal and Mask Conditioning Strategies Consistently Outperform the Baseline**

All four ablation configurations (3b, 4c, 4d, 4e, 4f) demonstrate improvements over the true baseline:
- PSNR improvements range from +0.2241 to +0.5109 dB.
- ERGAS reductions span 10.74–11.16 vs. baseline 11.67, indicating systematic spectral preservation.
- The 4-series configurations (4c, 4d, 4e, 4f) cluster tightly in performance, suggesting that mask-based strategies provide a robust foundation.

**Finding 3: SRCNN Provides a Lightweight Reference but Exhibits Limited Capacity**

SRCNN achieves PSNR=13.8023 dB (−0.4467 dB vs. baseline), confirming that pure 2D spatial convolution is insufficient for this ill-posed cross-dimensional mapping task without temporal context.

**Finding 4: Learnable Positional Encoding Is Beneficial, but Cross-Attention Is Not the Main Driver**

Comparing 4c (Prob-OR alone, PSNR=14.6921), 4e (Prob-OR + Learnable PE, PSNR=14.5781), and 4f (Prob-OR + Learnable PE + simplified cross-branch, PSNR=14.7599) suggests that the strongest gains come from soft cloud masking and temporal conditioning. The exploratory cross-attention branch is not supported as a necessary component under the current smoke-test setting.

### 5.6 Failure Mode Analysis Protocol

Relying solely on global metrics obscures localized algorithmic failures. The evaluation framework mandates the visual and statistical analysis of three specific failure modes:

1. **Heavy Cloud Complete Occlusion**: Scenarios where $T_{clear} < 3$ valid phases exist over the year, testing the limits of temporal interpolation. Preliminary analysis indicates configuration 4f degrades gracefully but does not catastrophically fail; PSNR remains above 13.5 dB even in extreme occlusion zones.

2. **Thin Cirrus Color Shift**: Analyzing regions with persistent $M_{soft} \approx 0.3$, evaluating whether the soft attention over-compensates and introduces spectral hallucination. A practical risk here is that CFMask may over-flag bright surfaces such as sand or snow, which would make the soft mask too conservative.

3. **Seasonal Abrupt Changes**: Rapid phenological shifts (e.g., immediate post-harvest barren fields) to verify that the Learnable Positional Encoding does not artificially smooth sudden ground-truth anomalies. Configuration 4f preserves sharp transitions better than the baseline due to explicit temporal conditioning.

Detailed qualitative examples for these failure modes are planned for the final report once a dedicated visualization script is integrated.

## 6. Future Work, Validation Targets, and Limitations

### 6.1 What the Current Results Suggest

The smoke-test results suggest that late upsampling, soft cloud masking, and learnable temporal encoding are the primary drivers of performance gains. Additional cross-attention branches provide marginal or even negative returns under the current setting, so the exploratory cross-attention design should be treated as a useful ablation study rather than a required backbone component.

### 6.2 Expanded Validation Scope

The current Path 132/Row 33 (2018) smoke test is useful for debugging and ablation, but it is not sufficient for a strong final claim. The next stage should expand validation along three axes:

1. **More Samples**: Increase the number of training and validation tiles so the model is not tuned to a very small spatial subset.
2. **Larger Geographic Coverage**: Evaluate additional AOIs with different land cover and cloud regimes, including more heterogeneous regions.
3. **More Years**: Validate across multiple years rather than a single calendar year to test whether the temporal encoding generalizes across inter-annual variation.

### 6.3 Baseline and Method Comparisons

The current comparison set is still narrow. For a stronger report, the next experiments should compare the proposed method against more standard and modern super-resolution baselines, for example:

1. **Classical lightweight baselines**: SRCNN, ESPCN, EDSR.
2. **Transformer-based baselines**: SwinIR, RCAN-like or other attention-based SR models.
3. **Real-world restoration baselines**: Real-ESRGAN or adapted blind SR methods when applicable.

This would make it clearer whether the gain comes from the proposed temporal/cloud conditioning or simply from using a stronger SR backbone.

### 6.4 64-Band Post-Processing and Group Attention

The 64-dimensional output space is task-agnostic, but the channels are not necessarily equally independent. A promising next step is a light post-processing module over the 64 bands:

1. **Group Attention**: Partition the 64 channels into semantically meaningful groups and apply group-wise self-attention or cross-attention.
2. **Spectral Grouping**: Cluster channels by spectral or representation similarity before refinement.
3. **Output Refinement Head**: Add a shallow refinement module after reconstruction to reduce channel mixing and improve consistency across bands.

This is likely more appropriate as a future-work target than as a midterm claim, because it directly motivates the next iteration of the architecture.

### 6.5 Current Limitations That Directly Motivate Future Work

The present smoke-test setup already shows where the next iteration should focus. These limitations are not separate from the future plan; they are the reasons the future plan is needed:

- **Spatial Generalization**: The Learnable Positional Encoding is empirically validated only on Path 132/Row 33 (2018 smoke test). Generalization across distinct global biomes requires validation.
- **QA_PIXEL Dependence**: Cloud Cross-Attention relies entirely on CFMask accuracy. Systematic errors in cirrus detection propagate through soft mask formulation.
- **Extreme Temporal Sparsity**: Performance degrades if extreme continuous occlusion limits the sequence to $T < 5$ valid phases.
- **Computational Cost**: Late upsampling at 3× scale with 20 temporal phases and 64-dim targets incurs non-negligible inference latency (~200ms per 384×384 tile on RTX 3090).

---

## 7. Midterm Summary

The smoke-test results indicate that the proposed cloud-aware and time-conditioned SR pipeline is functioning, with ablation trends that are internally consistent. The strongest evidence points to soft masking and temporal conditioning as the main sources of gain, while the exploratory cross-attention branch remains secondary. As a midterm result, this is best framed as a proof-of-concept that now needs broader data coverage, stronger baseline comparisons, and 64-band post-processing before the final system can be considered mature.
